# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Privacy-conscious general users who want to understand a site's network behavior without opening browser developer tools. They use DomainScan while browsing, shopping, reading news, or watching media, often with divided attention between the page and a narrow Chrome side panel. Their primary job is to identify exact destination domains and IP addresses, then copy only the subset they need.

## Product Purpose

DomainScan continuously records the network destinations observed for a browser tab and turns them into a calm, inspectable, copyable record. It exists to make otherwise invisible external connections understandable to non-specialists while preserving exact technical evidence for advanced users. Success means a user can immediately see that capture is active, understand what was observed, and copy domains, IP addresses, or selected rows without ambiguity.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, and protective. The product should feel like a trustworthy browser instrument: observant without being alarmist, technically credible without looking like developer tooling, and friendly without becoming playful.

## Anti-references

- Cybersecurity theatre, hacker-terminal styling, fluorescent threat colors, or fear-based scores.
- Generic AI dashboard patterns: oversized metrics, card grids, glass surfaces, gradients, decorative charts, and excessive rounding.
- Workflows split into artificial stages, funnels, or grouped summaries that hide the exact destination list.
- Ambiguous icon-only copy actions, one global “copy everything” action, or controls that make users guess whether they copy domains, IP addresses, or selected rows.
- Interfaces that imply every third-party request is malicious or fingerprinting detection is certain.

## Design Principles

1. Make the exact evidence primary; interpretation remains secondary and explicitly qualified.
2. Keep copying obvious and local: domains, IP addresses, and selected rows are distinct targets.
3. Accumulate continuously without visual churn or silent data loss.
4. Use progressive disclosure for technical detail, but never hide the destination itself.
5. Prefer familiar controls and a flat, scannable information hierarchy over decorative containers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard-only operation, visible focus, reduced motion, sufficient contrast, non-color-only status communication, and resilient layouts for English and Russian text expansion. Use English for non-Russian browser locales and Russian for Russian locales.
